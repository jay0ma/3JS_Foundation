Core / Rim

A small low-poly car on a single island — an art piece about being seen as one thing while living as many. Drive inward, past the chaos and the winding path, to the structured core. Eight monuments await. They have questions.

W A S D drive  ·  SPACE brake  ·  audio synthesized at runtime
Monument I — The Audio Pillar

The headphones, the cables, the rabbit hole.

I own three cables that measure nearly identical. I can tell you the soundstage width of the Simgot EW300 versus the Zero:Red. My friends call it obsessive. But there's something almost meditative about it — like studying a painting, except you live inside the painting. Is it a problem, or is it how some of us love music?

Monument II — The Structural Pillar

The invisible architecture.

React Native. Vercel. AeroSpace. My code is in your pocket, your browser, your commute. And I shipped it from my apartment, alone, probably at 2am. Society says programmers are basement hermits. Maybe we're just building things that outgrow the rooms they came from.

Monument III — The Cultural Pillar

The model minority myth, and the cost of performing it.

People assume I'm good at math before I open my mouth. They assume I'm quiet because I'm Asian. When I do speak up, I'm "different." When I don't, I'm "typical." I didn't ask to be a stereotype. I just wanted to exist without having to represent an entire race.

Monument IV — The Achievement Pillar

The grade, the worth, and the silence between.

My transcript is clean. My sense of self is not. Somewhere along the way, "being good at school" became the only dimension anyone cared about. Now I code at 3am chasing that same validation from a different authority — shipped commits, not report cards.

Monument V — The Gamer Pillar

The rank, the grind, and what nobody talks about.

I hit Grandmaster. Then I hit a wall. Turns out climbing the ladder feels a lot like escaping something. Gaming was my safe world — where the rules were clear and skill mattered. Real life doesn't have an ELO. It took me a long time to admit that being good at a game didn't teach me how to be good at being a person.

Monument VI — The Imposter Pillar

The 50k-user delusion.

50,000 people opened my app today. And I sat there thinking: when are they going to figure out I'm just guessing? The code works but I don't know why half of it works. I'm either genuinely good or the luckiest fraud alive. Most days I can't tell the difference. Maybe that's just what shipping things feels like.

Monument VII — The Musician Pillar

The genre nobody respects, and the joy nobody can take.

My parents wanted classical. I found electronic. They don't understand how sitting at a piano — with a midi controller and four synthesizers — is still playing piano. Music school kids look at me sideways. Tech kids think it's a hobby. It's neither. It's the most honest thing I do.

Monument VIII — The Creator Pillar

The builder, and the builder's builder, and the builder's builder's builder.

I make things that help people feel less alone. I shipped a meditation app. I still can't meditate. I built a social platform that helps people make friends. I have three close ones. There's a strange guilt in being the person who builds the raft while sitting in the water alone. Maybe the raft is enough. Maybe I need to swim too.
